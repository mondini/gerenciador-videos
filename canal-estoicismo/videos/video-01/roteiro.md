# Vídeo 1 — The Man Who Had Everything

- **Título de publicação:** The Richest Man in Rome Wrote This to Himself Every Night
- **Nome interno:** The Man Who Had Everything
- **Tema:** poder e humildade; o "suficiente" que nunca chega
- **Público-alvo do vídeo:** a pessoa comum que tem muito e sente que não é o bastante
- **Duração-alvo:** ~10 minutos (decisão do criador — não encorpar)
- **Provérbios-ponte:** 30:8-9 (bloco 4) e 16:18-19 (bloco 5)
- **Visão do criador (virada do bloco 4):** dinheiro não é o vilão; não ter dinheiro também faz infeliz; o problema é o "suficiente" que nunca chega. "Não é capitalismo malvado, é a sociedade atual."

## Bloco 1 — Gancho (aprovado)

```
[softly] Maybe you already have more than you once dreamed of.
A roof over your head. Light at the touch of a finger. Food you never had to hunt.
And still... at night, something whispers... [whispers] not yet. Not enough.

[thoughtful] Now imagine a man who truly had everything.
Every coin in the empire. Every army. Every door... open.
And yet, night after night, alone in a tent at the edge of the world... he wrote to himself.
Not about how to get more.
About how not to lose himself... in what he already had.
```

**Cenas (padrão de sombras, aprovado):** o homem entra caminhando; o templo/casa sobe do chão; a lamparina a óleo desce do teto e acende (chama âmbar); a mesa com tigela, pão e ânfora desliza da direita; "not yet... not enough" escrito na tela + noite cai + lua + **foco de luz e zoom no homem curvado**. Luz forte: o imperador sobe num pedestal, moedas chovem, soldados marcham dos dois lados, o arco se abre. Noite à beira do mundo: penhasco, tenda, ciprestes, o imperador sentado escrevendo, fumaça da lamparina, **foco de luz e zoom lento** até o fim do bloco.

## Bloco 2 — O homem (aprovado)

```
[thoughtful] His name was Marcus Aurelius.
For nearly twenty years, he ruled Rome... at a time when Rome was, for many, the whole known world.

But his years on the throne were not years of comfort.
A plague swept across the empire... city after city.
And on the northern frontier, along the river Danube, the wars would not end.
So the most powerful man alive spent his nights not in a palace... but in a soldier's camp. Cold. Far from home. [sighs]

And when he sat down to write... he did not write about his victories.
He wrote about the people who had shaped him.
And of his mother, he remembered this:
[softly] simplicity in my way of living... far removed from the habits of the rich.

Think about that.
The richest man in the world... grateful that someone taught him not to live like a rich man.

The old historians even tell a story.
When the wars emptied the treasury, he did not raise taxes on his people.
He sold the treasures of his own palace instead.
Whether every detail is true... we cannot know.
[warmly] But the story survived for a reason. It sounds exactly like the man who wrote those pages.
```

**Ideias de cena (adaptar ao padrão de sombras):** nome em Cinzel com coroa de louros; templo que racha; peste = máscara de bico (anacrônica: séc. XVII — é simbólica; alternativa histórica: cidades se apagando) e janelas de cidades se apagando; Danúbio correndo, escudo e lanças; acampamento com fogueira e neve no "Cold"; pena escrevendo; bustos das pessoas que o formaram, o da mãe destacado; citação na tela; pergaminho se desenrolando (lenda); baú de onde os tesouros saem.
**Créditos na tela:** *Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 1.3 · trans. George Long* · *Historia Augusta, Life of Marcus 17*

## Bloco 3 — O diário (aprovado)

```
[thoughtful] What he wrote in that tent was never meant for us.
He did not write it in Latin, the language of the empire... but in Greek. The language of philosophy. The language of his teachers.
And the title these pages have carried for centuries is simply this:
To Himself.

No audience. No applause. No one to impress.
Just a tired man... reminding himself, again and again, of the person he wanted to be.

And that is what makes it so honest.
Because he repeats himself.
He writes the same warnings over and over... the way you only repeat what you keep forgetting.
[softly] This was not a perfect man writing down his perfection.
This was a man fighting, every single day... not to be ruined by what he had.

Some mornings, he prepared himself for the people he would face:
Begin the morning by saying to thyself... I shall meet with the busy-body, the ungrateful, arrogant, deceitful, envious, unsocial.
[warmly] In other words... before the day began, he already knew that difficult people would cross his path.
He did not expect a world without them. He only refused to let them steal his peace.

And some nights... he wrote a warning that only an emperor could write.
A warning about himself.
[softly] Take care that thou art not made into a Caesar... that thou art not dyed with this dye. For such things happen.
[thoughtful] In simple words... be careful not to become only the title. Only the crown. With no man left inside it.

Dyed... like the purple robes of the emperors.
The color of power... soaking into a man, slowly... until there is nothing of him left underneath.
```

**Ideias de cena:** latim riscado → ΦΙΛΟΣΟΦΙΑ + mestres; título ΤΑ ΕΙΣ ΕΑΥΤΟΝ / *To Himself*; anfiteatro vazio; a mesma linha escrita seis vezes e se apagando; busto com rachadura âmbar; amanhecer + citação 2.1 + fileira de pessoas, ele em destaque; noite + lua + citação 6.30; coroa que fica enquanto o homem embaixo some; manto tingido de cima para baixo.
**Créditos:** *Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 2.1 · trans. George Long* · *Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 6.30 · trans. George Long*

## Bloco 4 — A lição (aprovado)

```
[thoughtful] Now... think about your own life for a moment.
Remember the day you held a new phone for the first time.
The smooth glass. The speed. The small thrill of something new in your hands.
And then... a few months later... it was just a phone.
Slower than the new one. Older than the one you saw in someone else's hand.
Nothing changed in the phone. Something changed in you.

[warmly] Now, let me be honest with you.
Money is not the enemy.
Not having enough is real. It keeps people awake at night. It breaks families. It takes away choices.
There is no wisdom in pretending otherwise.
[softly] But there is another kind of poverty... the one that follows you no matter how much you earn.
The feeling that enough... is always one step ahead.

In the book of Proverbs, there is a prayer that asks for something strange:
Give me neither poverty nor riches. Feed me with the food that is needful for me.
[warmly] In other words... not so poor that I am pushed to do wrong. And not so rich... that I forget who gave me everything.

[thoughtful] Marcus Aurelius left himself a simple exercise for this.
Think not so much of what thou hast not as of what thou hast.
In simple words... stop counting what is missing. Look at what is already here.
And then he goes one step further.
Choose the best of the things you have... and imagine how much you would long for them, if they were gone.

[softly] Let's pause here.
```

**Ideias de cena:** celular que "envelhece" e é empurrado por um novo maior; figura com redemoinho sobre a cabeça; moedas, boleto, lua da insônia, família com rachadura, portas fechando. **A escada (figura subindo com a bandeira "enough") foi REPROVADA — criar outra metáfora para "another kind of poverty... enough is always one step ahead".** Balança que se equilibra no provérbio; na 7.27, a casa, a lâmpada e a tigela do gancho voltam em âmbar, somem no "if they were gone" e reaparecem.
**Créditos:** *Proverbs 30:8 · World English Bible* · *Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 7.27 · trans. George Long*

## The Pause (a reescrever)

Feedback do criador: estava **longa demais** e **não funcionava para quem só ouve** (sem instrução nem pergunta faladas).
Diretriz para a nova versão: o narrador diz "Let's pause here", dá uma instrução curta de respiração em voz alta (ex.: inspirar 4 s, expirar 6 s, um a dois ciclos guiados) e **lê a pergunta**: *What do you have today... that you once prayed for?* Duração total bem menor que os ~40 s da prévia. Visual: o sol do logo respirando (sobe na inspiração, desce na expiração), adaptado ao lençol de sombras; capítulo próprio "The Pause".

## Bloco 5 — Provérbios 16:18 (aprovado)

```
[thoughtful] Far from Rome... in another land, and another time... a much older book had already written down the same warning.
Tradition says these words came from a king of Israel. Solomon.
And in one single line... he holds what Marcus spent years trying to remember:
Pride goes before destruction, and an arrogant spirit before a fall.
[warmly] In other words... the higher you climb inside your own head, the harder the ground waits for you.

And the verse that follows goes even further:
It is better to be of a lowly spirit with the poor, than to divide the plunder with the proud.
In simple words... better to have less and keep your soul... than to have more and lose it.

[softly] Two men who never met.
One, a king of Israel. The other, an emperor of Rome.
Different faiths. Different languages. Centuries apart.
And yet... both of them arrived at the same quiet truth.
Power does not ruin a man all at once.
It whispers to him, slowly... that he is more than he is.
```

**Ideias de cena:** rolo hebraico do lado oposto ao pergaminho romano; figura que sobe uma torre e despenca no "fall"; duas silhuetas (louros × coroa real) nos extremos, cada uma com uma linha âmbar que se encontra no centro em "the same quiet truth".
**Créditos:** *Proverbs 16:18 · World English Bible* · *Proverbs 16:19 · World English Bible*
