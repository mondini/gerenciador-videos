Stoicism has a surprising answer about anger: anger isn't the problem. The grudge is. The argument you keep winning in your head, night after night, is the part that stays.

About a hundred years before Marcus Aurelius, Seneca wrote an entire book about anger, and noticed that we don't just feel it, we feed it. In this slow, calm episode we read what Seneca, Marcus Aurelius and the book of Proverbs say about the boss who never gives you credit, the relative who always wants one more favor, and the replay that keeps you awake at 3 a.m.

Then the story at the heart of the episode: in the year 175, Marcus Aurelius was betrayed by one of his most trusted generals. When the rebellion ended, he was handed the letters of everyone who had supported the traitor. What he did with them says more about self-control than any victory.
