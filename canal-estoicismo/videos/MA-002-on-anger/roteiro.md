# Vídeo 2 — On Anger

- **Título de publicação (plano):** How to Stay Calm When Work and Money Push You to the Edge
- **Nome interno:** On Anger
- **Formato:** Problema do dia a dia (dor concreta de trabalho ou dinheiro → resposta estoica)
- **Tema:** a ira como fraqueza, não força; "quem te irrita tem poder sobre você"
- **Exemplo prático (plano):** reunião tensa, cliente que desrespeita
- **Provérbio-ponte (plano):** 16:32
- **Palavras-chave:** stoicism anger, controlling emotions, marcus aurelius anger, self control, calm mind
- **Duração-alvo:** ~10 minutos (~1.300–1.500 palavras faladas)
- **Visão do criador (13/10/2026):** o exemplo é o **cliente folgado**: já fazemos mais do que o necessário, ele não percebe e sempre quer mais. **A raiva é natural; o problema é o rancor que sobra por dias depois.** Provérbios que ajudem a sair disso pelo pensamento, **sem se basear em fé** (nada de "espere no Senhor").
- **Título (escolhido em 13/10/2026):** **Your Anger Isn't the Problem. The Grudge Is.** (44 caracteres). Substitui o título do plano a pedido do criador: enquadramento negativo, mais sensacionalista, dentro de 30–50 caracteres. Alternativa para o teste A/B: *Never Let a Difficult Client Live in Your Head*.
- **Público (13/10/2026):** gancho genérico, com problemas com chefe, mãe, pai, amigos; **não** só cliente, para não fechar o funil em empresários. Sêneca III.43 fica fora do bloco 3 (complexo de entender).
- **Fontes validadas (13/10/2026):** Sêneca (Stewart, 1889) e Cássio Dio (Loeb 1927, contado com nossas palavras). Registradas na regra 1 de `docs/plano-canal.md`.

## Bloco 2 — A raiva e o rancor (aprovado)

```
[thoughtful] About a hundred years before Marcus, another Roman wrote an entire book about anger.
His name was Seneca.
And he noticed something we rarely admit.
We don't just feel anger... we feed it.
Even when we begin to suspect it was something small... we keep going. Because stopping would mean admitting we were angry for nothing.
He put it like this:
we nurse it and inflame it, as though to be violently angry proved our anger to be just.
[warmly] In other words... we keep the fire burning, just to prove we were right to light it.

[thoughtful] Marcus knew the same trap.
In one of the longest entries of his journal, he left himself a list of rules... for the days when someone wronged him.
And one of them says this:
consider how much more pain is brought on us by the anger and vexation caused by such acts than by the acts themselves.
[softly] In simple words... what they did to you happened once.
What you do with it... happens every day.

The insult took a few seconds.
The grudge... can take years.

[thoughtful] The book of Proverbs has an image for this.
A stone is heavy, and sand is a burden; but a fool's provocation is heavier than both.
[warmly] In other words... one careless comment can weigh more than everything you lift at the gym.
And unlike the gym... carrying it won't make you any stronger.
[softly] And still... most of us carry it.
Home. To the dinner table. And into bed.
```

Toque leve de humor na academia a pedido do criador (13/10/2026).
**Short:** "In simple words... what they did to you happened once." até "...can take years."
**Ideias de cena:** fogueira sendo alimentada (lenha a cada "we feed it"); a lista de regras de Marco escrita em coluna; a pedra pesando nas costas da silhueta; academia em sombras (halteres) na piada; a silhueta com a pedra chega à mesa de jantar e à cama.
**Créditos:** *Seneca, Of Anger III.29 · trans. Aubrey Stewart* · *Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 11.18 · trans. George Long* · *Proverbs 27:3 · World English Bible*

## Bloco 3 — A pessoa que sempre quer mais (aprovado)

```
[thoughtful] And sometimes... it isn't one careless comment.
It's a person.
The boss who presents your work in the meeting... as if it were his own. And never once says your name.
The relative who always expects one more favor.
The friend who only calls... when they need something.
You give more than you should. And still... they want more.

[softly] Marcus had people like that around him too. Every single day.
And he left himself a simple question for those moments.
When someone's behavior offends you... ask yourself: could the world exist without people like this?
It couldn't. So stop demanding the impossible.
[warmly] Expecting a world without them... is like getting angry at winter for being cold.
[thoughtful] That doesn't mean they're right.
It means you stop waiting for them to change... before you allow yourself some peace.

[thoughtful] And here is where anger can actually help you.
Anger is a signal. It tells you a line was crossed.
In another entry, Marcus asks himself a blunt question:
why not act... instead of complain?
So act.
Say no. Ask for that conversation. Draw the line... out loud.
[warmly] And Proverbs has some surprising advice on how to do it:
By patience a ruler is persuaded. A soft tongue breaks the bone.
The calm voice is not the weak one.
Sometimes it's the only voice strong enough to move a stubborn man.

[softly] That is the difference.
Anger, used well... becomes an action. A boundary. A conversation.
A grudge becomes nothing.
It just sits there... and waits for you, every night.
```

Exemplo de trabalho: o chefe que apresenta o seu trabalho como dele e não dá crédito (pedido do criador). Primeira aplicação da regra 3 revisada: 9.42 e 8.47 parafraseados, só o 25:15 literal. O 8.47, no original, trata do que nos incomoda em geral; por isso "another entry" + "blunt question", sem dizer que fala de pessoas.
**Short:** "Anger, used well... becomes an action." até "...every night."
**Ideias de cena:** reunião em sombras, o chefe ergue o papel/trabalho da silhueta e recebe os aplausos; parente e amigo com as mãos estendidas; inverno, neve caindo sobre uma silhueta irritada; uma linha traçada no chão em âmbar no "Draw the line"; a voz baixa como onda suave que move uma pedra; o rancor sentado à beira da cama esperando.
**Créditos:** *Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 9.42 · trans. George Long* · *Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 8.47 · trans. George Long* · *Proverbs 25:15 · World English Bible*

## Bloco 4 — A traição (aprovado)

```
[thoughtful] Now... let me tell you what happened to Marcus.
In the year 175, one of his most trusted generals was a man named Avidius Cassius.
A hero of the wars in the East. Marcus had placed the entire East in his hands.
Then a false rumor reached him... that the emperor was dead.
And Cassius declared himself emperor.
[softly] Imagine hearing that.
The man you raised up... now calling himself by your title.
Rome began to prepare for civil war.

[thoughtful] Marcus gathered his soldiers. And the old historian Cassius Dio tells us what he said.
He was afraid of only one thing.
Not defeat. Not death.
He was afraid that Cassius would die... before Marcus had the chance to forgive him.
To forgive a man who wronged you, he said... that would be a greater prize than any victory.

[softly] He never got that chance.
Three months later, Cassius was killed by his own officers. They brought his head to the emperor... like a gift.
Marcus would not look at it. He ordered it buried.
And he spared almost everyone who had joined the revolt.

[thoughtful] And then... the letters.
Among the rebels' papers, there were letters. Names. Every man who had written in support of the traitor.
Any other ruler would have read every line.
Marcus burned them. Without reading a single one.
Because he did not want to learn their names... and be forced to hate them.
One old account says a governor burned them in his place. But it is Marcus's reason that survived.

[warmly] Think about that.
He didn't wait for the hatred to come... and then fight it.
He simply refused to feed it.
In his journal, he wrote:
as the sense of pain is a characteristic of weakness, so also is anger.
Anger looks like strength. It isn't. It's a wound.
[softly] And the most powerful man in the world knew... some letters are better left unread.

[softly] Let's pause here.
```

História de Cássio Dio contada com nossas palavras (Loeb 1927); fora a fofoca sobre Faustina. Única citação literal: 11.18. Fecha o *loop* do gancho; "refused to feed it" prepara o Provérbios 26:20 do bloco 5.
**Short (o mais forte):** "And then... the letters." até "...be forced to hate them."
**Ideias de cena:** mapa com o Oriente se acendendo em âmbar nas mãos do general; mensageiro a cavalo com o boato; a silhueta de Cássio erguendo uma coroa; legiões se alinhando; Marco falando aos soldados; a cabeça trazida numa caixa/pano (nunca mostrada: Marco vira o rosto); baú de cartas, rolos com selos; as cartas no fogo, ainda fechadas, virando cinza; coluna/lista do diário com a citação.
**Créditos:** *Cassius Dio, Roman History 72.26–29 · Loeb Classical Library* · *Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 11.18 · trans. George Long*

## The Pause (aprovado)

```
[softly] Seneca wrote that the greatest remedy for anger... is delay.
So let's take a small delay. Right now.
Unclench your jaw. Let your hands fall open.
Breathe in... slowly... two... three... four.
And let it go... two... three... four... five... six.
One more time.
Breathe in... two... three... four.
And out... two... three... four... five... six.
[warmly] Now... one question. Just for you.
What are you still carrying... that happened days ago?
[softly] You don't have to put it down yet.
Just notice... how heavy it is.
```

~30 s, mesmo formato do MA-001 (contagem falada, pergunta lida), com o corpo da raiva (maxilar, mãos) e a pergunta ligada à pedra do bloco 2. Soltar o peso fica para o fechamento.
**Ideias de cena:** no lugar do sol do logo, **uma brasa** que acende na inspiração e esfria na expiração (mesmo formato, outra animação); a pergunta na tela em Cormorant Garamond itálico.
**Créditos:** *Seneca, Of Anger II.29 · trans. Aubrey Stewart*

## Bloco 5 — Provérbio-ponte (aprovado)

```
[thoughtful] Marcus took cities. He won wars at the edge of the empire.
But the victory that says the most about him... is a box of letters he never opened.
The book of Proverbs, written centuries before him, already knew which victory matters more:
One who is slow to anger is better than the mighty; one who rules his spirit, than he who takes a city.
[warmly] Taking a city takes an army.
Ruling your own spirit... you do alone. Every day. And usually at night.

[thoughtful] And there is another proverb... one of the simplest in the whole book.
Without wood... a fire goes out.
That's all. No magic. No great effort.
[softly] A grudge is a fire.
And every time you replay the scene... every time you tell the story again... every time you rehearse what you should have said...
you add another piece of wood.
You don't have to fight the fire.
You only have to stop feeding it.
[warmly] It won't go out tonight. Grudges are stubborn.
But a fire with no wood... can only do one thing.
[softly] Slowly... it turns to ash.
```

Literal só o 16:32; o 26:20 simplificado ("tell the story again" vem da 2ª metade: "Without gossip, a quarrel dies down"). Sem atribuir a Salomão e sem o "two men who never met" do MA-001.
**Short:** "A grudge is a fire." até "...stop feeding it."
**Ideias de cena:** cidade murada tomada por legiões × silhueta sozinha à noite (governar a si mesmo); caixa de cartas fechada em âmbar; fogueira que cresce a cada "every time…" (lenha jogada três vezes); a mão que segura a lenha e não joga; o fogo baixa até virar cinza.
**Créditos:** *Proverbs 16:32 · World English Bible* · *Proverbs 26:20 · World English Bible*

## Bloco 6 — Fechamento (aprovado)

```
[softly] So... tonight, if that argument starts again in your head...
the one you've already won a hundred times...
you don't have to win it one more time.
You don't have to like them. You don't have to pretend they were right.
[warmly] When the next replay begins... you can simply let it pass.
And leave the fire... unfed.
Let the conversation end... without you.
[thoughtful] Some of them will never say sorry. Some will never even know.
That was never the point.
Your peace was always yours to keep.

[softly] Almost two thousand years ago, an emperor let a box of letters burn.
He never learned the names.
He never had to carry them.
[thoughtful] And somewhere in those ashes... one small ember is still glowing.
Not with anger.
Only... with warmth.

[softly] Old wisdom... for quiet hours.
```

"Notice the wood in your hand" trocado por "let it pass / leave the fire unfed" (duplo sentido em inglês). Encerramento padrão do canal.
**Short (obrigatório, pedido do criador):** "So... tonight, if that argument starts again in your head..." até "...without you." Frase-chave: *you don't have to win it one more time*.
**Ideias de cena:** a silhueta de punhos fechados discute com uma sombra; em "you don't have to win it one more time" ela **solta os ombros e abre as mãos**, a sombra do outro se desfaz e a luz âmbar da sala amolece (**alívio extremo**, peso saindo do corpo); o próximo "replay" passa como fumaça sem chegar ao fogo; a caixa de cartas queimando, as cinzas; uma única brasa âmbar brilhando, calor e não raiva; escurece; encerramento padrão.
**Créditos:** nenhum (texto do canal).

## Plano de arte (aprovado em 13/10/2026)

Critério: o que é identidade da série volta; objetos marcantes e composições de cena do MA-001 não se repetem.

- **Moldura:** "oliveira" (`cenario.laterais_oliveira`, `cenario.base_lenha`, `cenario.cidade`, `cenario.vinhedo`, `PANO = 'pesado'`). Lua sempre crescente ou minguante.
- **Reaproveitar como está:** imperador; **homem comum** (o mesmo do MA-001, personagem recorrente "o ouvinte"); letreiros, créditos, lua, foco, zoom, chamas; flocos de neve (bloco 3); coroa de louros (Cássio se coroando, bloco 4); **rolo hebraico como ícone fixo de Provérbios**, pequeno, num canto, junto da citação (nunca a cena grande do MA-001).
- **Reaproveitar com outra cara:** soldados (dois exércitos parados frente a frente, com estandartes, sem marcha); chamas (fogueira de lenha cruzada no chão, que cresce a cada tora).
- **Arte nova:**
  - Bloco 1: duas silhuetas discutindo; a sombra do outro voltando em loop (banho/bica, carroça na estrada, cama às 3h).
  - Bloco 2: fogueira alimentada; tabuinha de cera com a lista de regras; a pedra nas costas; halteres antigos (piada da academia); mesa de jantar simples (não a de pés de leão).
  - Bloco 3: reunião (o chefe ergue o trabalho e recebe os aplausos); mãos estendidas; linha âmbar no chão; a voz calma como onda que move a pedra; o rancor sentado à beira da cama.
  - Bloco 4: mapa do Oriente; mensageiro a cavalo; caixa coberta (Marco vira o rosto); capsa (estojo de rolos com selos) no lugar do baú; cartas lacradas queimando até a cinza.
  - Pausa: a brasa que respira (no lugar do sol/mar do logo).
  - Bloco 5: cidade murada cercada × silhueta sozinha; a mão que segura a tora e não joga; fogo baixando até a cinza.
  - Bloco 6: o alívio (punhos que abrem, ombros que caem, a sombra do outro se desfaz); pensamento que passa como fumaça; a última brasa nas cinzas.
