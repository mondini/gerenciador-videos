# Vídeo 2 — On Anger

- **Título de publicação (plano):** How to Stay Calm When Work and Money Push You to the Edge
- **Nome interno:** On Anger
- **Formato:** Problema do dia a dia (dor concreta de trabalho ou dinheiro → resposta estoica)
- **Tema:** a ira como fraqueza, não força; "quem te irrita tem poder sobre você"
- **Exemplo prático (plano):** reunião tensa, cliente que desrespeita
- **Provérbio-ponte (plano):** 16:32
- **Palavras-chave:** stoicism anger, controlling emotions, marcus aurelius anger, self control, calm mind
- **Duração-alvo:** ~10 minutos (~1.300–1.500 palavras faladas)
- **Visão do criador (13/10/2026):** o exemplo é o **cliente folgado**: já fazemos mais do que o necessário, ele não percebe e sempre quer mais. **A raiva é natural; o problema é o rancor que sobra por dias depois.** Provérbios que ajudem a sair disso pelo pensamento, **sem se basear em fé** (nada de "espere no Senhor").
- **Título (escolhido em 13/10/2026):** **Your Anger Isn't the Problem. The Grudge Is.** (44 caracteres). Substitui o título do plano a pedido do criador: enquadramento negativo, mais sensacionalista, dentro de 30–50 caracteres. Alternativa para o teste A/B: *Never Let a Difficult Client Live in Your Head*.
- **Público (13/10/2026):** gancho genérico, com problemas com chefe, mãe, pai, amigos; **não** só cliente, para não fechar o funil em empresários. Sêneca III.43 fica fora do bloco 3 (complexo de entender).
- **Fontes validadas (13/10/2026):** Sêneca (Stewart, 1889) e Cássio Dio (Loeb 1927, contado com nossas palavras). Registradas na regra 1 de `docs/plano-canal.md`.

## Bloco 1 — Gancho (aprovado)

```
[softly] You've already won this argument... a hundred times.
In the shower. On the drive home. At three in the morning, staring at the ceiling.
You know exactly what you should have said.
To your boss. To your mother. To that person who always wants a little more... than you can give.
[sighs] And every time you win it... you lose a little more of your peace.
[thoughtful] Here is the strange part.
Your anger is not the problem. Anger is human. It comes... and it goes.
The problem is the grudge. The part that stays.
The Stoics understood this better than anyone.
And Marcus Aurelius, emperor of Rome, once faced the worst betrayal of his life...
and did something with the traitor's letters... that almost no one would do.
[softly] We'll get there.
```

## Bloco 2 — A raiva e o rancor (aprovado)

```
[thoughtful] About a hundred years before Marcus, another Roman wrote an entire book about anger.
His name was Seneca.
And he noticed something we rarely admit.
We don't just feel anger... we feed it.
Even when we begin to suspect it was something small... we keep going. Because stopping would mean admitting we were angry for nothing.
He put it like this:
we nurse it and inflame it, as though to be violently angry proved our anger to be just.
[warmly] In other words... we keep the fire burning, just to prove we were right to light it.

[thoughtful] Marcus knew the same trap.
In one of the longest entries of his journal, he left himself a list of rules... for the days when someone wronged him.
And one of them says this:
consider how much more pain is brought on us by the anger and vexation caused by such acts than by the acts themselves.
[softly] In simple words... what they did to you happened once.
What you do with it... happens every day.

The insult took a few seconds.
The grudge... can take years.

[thoughtful] The book of Proverbs has an image for this.
A stone is heavy, and sand is a burden; but a fool's provocation is heavier than both.
[warmly] In other words... one careless comment can weigh more than everything you lift at the gym.
And unlike the gym... carrying it won't make you any stronger.
[softly] And still... most of us carry it.
Home. To the dinner table. And into bed.
```

Toque leve de humor na academia a pedido do criador (13/10/2026).
**Short:** "In simple words... what they did to you happened once." até "...can take years."
**Ideias de cena:** fogueira sendo alimentada (lenha a cada "we feed it"); a lista de regras de Marco escrita em coluna; a pedra pesando nas costas da silhueta; academia em sombras (halteres) na piada; a silhueta com a pedra chega à mesa de jantar e à cama.
**Créditos:** *Seneca, Of Anger III.29 · trans. Aubrey Stewart* · *Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 11.18 · trans. George Long* · *Proverbs 27:3 · World English Bible*
